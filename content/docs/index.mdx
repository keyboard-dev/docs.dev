Most documentation tools give you a rectangle of text and an editor that looks
nothing like the page your readers see. docs.dev collapses that distance twice:
prose flows around figures the way a well-set magazine column does, and when
you hit *Edit*, the published page itself becomes the editor.

<Spread image="/uploads/flow-around-figure.webp" alt="Lines of text flowing around a glowing figure on both sides" side="right" width="38%" top={6}>
The layout engine under this page is [pretext](https://github.com/chenglou/pretext):
every run of text is measured with canvas arithmetic and placed as real,
selectable DOM text — on **both** sides of a figure at once, which CSS floats
and CSS Shapes cannot express. Resize the window and the paragraph you are
reading re-measures and re-breaks against the live column width. Search
engines and screen readers always get the same words in clean reading order,
because the flowed layout is a progressive enhancement over an ordinary
paragraph, not a hidden copy of it.
</Spread>

## Editing, without an editor

Sign in and every page grows an *Edit* button. Headings, prose, code, tables,
even the figure spreads above are editable in place — same column, same
typography, same rhythm you are reading now. Drafts autosave locally and share
to your team instantly, and publishing commits straight to GitHub with your
name on the commit.
