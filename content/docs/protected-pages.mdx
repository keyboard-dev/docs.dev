---
title: Protected pages
description: Lock individual pages behind a reading PIN or shareable invite links — without hiding them from your own team.
---

Some pages aren't for everyone: internal runbooks, pre-launch specs, notes
for a single customer. Mark a page **protected** and it stays on your site —
title and description visible in the sidebar — but its content is only served
to readers who can open it.

## Protect a page

In the editor, use the **Protect** toggle at the top of the page (next to
where the copy-markdown buttons appear on the published page), then publish.
The toggle writes `protected: true` into the page's frontmatter, so you can
also set it by hand or from an agent:

```mdx
---
title: Internal runbook
description: On-call procedures for the docs site.
protected: true
---
```

A protected page is built as a *lock shell*: the static HTML carries only the
title, description, and an unlock form — never the content. The content is
served exclusively by the Worker after it checks, on the server, that the
request is allowed. Signed-in editors and admins always see the page.

## Share it with an invite link

The lightest way to let someone in: next to the lock toggle, open
**Invite…**, pick how long the link should last (7, 30, or 90 days, or no
expiry), and the invite link is copied to your clipboard:

```
https://your-site.dev/docs/internal-runbook?invite=inv1.1767225600.9f2ab41c…
```

Send that link to whoever needs the page. Opening it unlocks **that one
page** in their browser — no PIN, no account — and stays unlocked across
refreshes and return visits, even after the `?invite=` part is dropped.

A few properties worth knowing:

- The hash is **bound to the page**: an invite minted for one page does
  nothing on any other page.
- Invites are **stateless** — nothing is stored server-side. There's no list
  of issued links and no per-link revocation; expiry is the blast-radius
  control, so prefer dated links for anything sensitive.
- Rotating the `ADMIN_SECRET` secret **revokes every invite link** (and every
  unlocked browser) at once.
- Invite links only need `ADMIN_SECRET` to be set — they work even if you
  never configure a reading PIN.

## Or hand out the reading PIN

For "everyone at the company may read these" situations, set a site-wide
reading PIN:

```bash
wrangler secret put READER_PIN
```

Readers hitting a protected page get a PIN form; a correct PIN unlocks **all
protected pages** in that browser for 30 days. The reading PIN grants reading
only — it's deliberately separate from `ADMIN_PIN`, which signs editors in.

With `READER_PIN` unset, protected pages fail closed: only signed-in team
members and invite-link holders can read them.

## What stays private

Protection is enforced at the content door, not just the page:

- **Search** still finds protected pages by title and description, but their
  body text never enters the search index.
- The raw-markdown mirrors (`.md` URLs, `/llms.mdx/...`) return 404 for
  protected pages, and `llms.txt` / `llms-full.txt` skip them entirely.
- The [AI assistant](/docs/ai) and the [MCP server](/docs/claude-code) can't
  see them — they're excluded from retrieval, search, and reading.

The title and one-line description remain public (they appear in the sidebar
and lock shell), so keep those unrevealing on genuinely sensitive pages.
